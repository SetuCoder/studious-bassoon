# Product background

As of 21 October 2026. The full team manual (17 sections: research, market, business model, risks) is a separate document; this file keeps only what the build needs.

## One line

A repayment optimiser for people juggling several loans and cards: see everything you owe in one place, get a ranked plan for your next rupee, and catch charges that do not add up.

## Who it is for

- **Salaried borrowers with two or more credit lines:** a home loan, a car or education loan, two or three cards, sometimes a personal loan or BNPL. Each lives in its own lender app.
- **Self-employed owners** whose personal and business debt are mixed (they often work through a CA).
- **Not for:** people who run everything on autopay and never make a debt decision. Two of our fifteen interviewees were like this.

## The problem

People can see every loan, but not what their options really cost. Autopay already solves due dates. The pain is the decision: when a bonus arrives, a cash gap opens, an offer lands or a loan is closed, they rebuild the numbers by hand and still doubt the answer.

From 15 borrower interviews:

| Finding | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Recently faced a decision about where money should go | 87% (13 of 15) |
| Hit a charge they did not expect or could not explain | 80% (12 of 15) |
| Keep a debt spreadsheet by hand (half let it go stale) | 40% (6 of 15) |
| Dropped a budgeting app because categorising felt like work | 27% (4 of 15) |
| Had a live view of all their debt | 0% |
| Pay for help with loan decisions | 0% |

## The four needs, and what the product does about each

| Need | Feature |
| --- | --- |
| 1. One trustworthy picture of what I owe | Debts ledger: every loan and card, totals, sorted by cost |
| 2. Knowing what to do with the next rupee | Engine that ranks options by net gain over 36 months against keeping the money in savings |
| 3. Confidence that charges are legitimate | Charge check against the RBI prepayment rule, with a drafted question for the lender |
| 4. No upkeep, and no handing over everything | Five-field quick-add, data stays on the phone, no bank login or SMS access |

## Principles

- **The engine computes; words only explain.** Every number comes from tested code and can be traced.
- **Liquidity is a real goal.** People often choose to keep cash over the cheapest option. The buffer the user keeps aside is never spent by any option.
- **Trust comes from visible working.** Show the calculation behind every recommendation.
- **Product-neutral.** Compare by cost. Never recommend a financial product. If we ever earn referral fees, they must be disclosed and must not change the ranking.
- **We never hold or move money, and never contact lenders.**

## Positioning against what people use

| Today | Falls short because |
| --- | --- |
| Lender and bank apps | One lender's slice; no comparison across loans |
| CRED and score apps | Built around taking on more credit, not deciding what to do with debt you hold |
| Spreadsheets | Manual upkeep; half go stale |
| CAs and planners | ₹1,500 to ₹7,500 a review, aimed at tax and investing |
| Budgeting apps | Track spending; categorising feels like work |

## The beta goal

30 real testers (people with two or more loans or cards) using the app with their own numbers, giving feedback. Key question asked in-app: "Would you act on this?"

## Later layers (not in the beta)

Statement upload and reading, offer comparison (balance transfers, refinancing), credit health, adviser tools for CAs and brokers, employer and bank distribution, card-for-purchase look-back, surplus redirect. Live bank linking needs RBI's Account Aggregator route and a regulated partner.
